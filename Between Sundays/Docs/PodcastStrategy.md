## Part One: What This Podcast Is (and Isn't)

This is not a sermon in audio form. It is not a Bible study. It is not a motivational
talk dressed in scripture.

It is two people, on record, being honest about the hardest questions real believers
face — and finding that God is already in the middle of them. The tone is a kitchen
table conversation, not a pulpit. The goal is not to fix anyone. It is to make the
listener feel less alone and more held.

Every episode will leave someone thinking: *That is exactly what I have been carrying
and couldn't say out loud.*

That is the bar.

---

## Part Two: Podcast Name

### BETWEEN SUNDAYS
*A GraceNotes Daily podcast*

**Why it works:** Most Christian content exists in the Sunday context — the sermon,
the worship, the together. This show lives in the Monday-through-Saturday space:
job loss, hospital waiting rooms, 3am anxiety, the argument you had in the car on
the way to church. The name tells the listener exactly where this podcast meets them,
without requiring any explanation. It's instantly clear this is a Christian show, and
it's instantly clear it's not a sermon.

**Tagline:** *Where faith meets real life — everything that happens between Sundays.*

**SEO angle:** "Christian podcast," "everyday faith," "Christian daily living,"
"faith in real life," "faith for hard seasons"

**Channel branding note:** *Between Sundays* lives as both the Spotify/Apple podcast
name and the YouTube channel name. The visual brand carries the GraceNotes palette:
warm, clean, unhurried. Show art should feel like a Sunday afternoon, not a stage.

## Part Three: The Host Personas

### Primary Host: ELI BENI
*The consistent, default voice of Between Sundays.*

**Who he is:** Eli is the kind of man who has read everything and lived through
enough that the two things have merged. He is theologically sharp but has no interest
in performing it. He has been disappointed by God — or thought he had — and found
his faith on the other side of that richer and less neat. He asks questions in public
that most Christians only ask in private. He is bold without being combative, warm
without being soft. He calls things what they are.

He is not a pastor. He might have been trained as one, or close to it. But his
seat is at the table, not the pulpit. His authority is earned by honesty, not by
title. He is the listener's trusted older brother in faith — the one who won't
protect you from a hard question, but will stay in the room while you sit with it.

**Voice quality for 11 Labs:** Warm baritone. Measured pace with the ability to
slow down into weight. Sounds like someone thinking out loud, not delivering a
prepared statement. The kind of voice that makes you turn the volume up, not down.
A man who pauses before he speaks because what he's about to say matters to him.

**His signature move in conversation:** When the guest says something real, Eli gets
quieter. He doesn't rush to fill the space. Then he names it — not explains it,
just names it. *"That's the thing nobody talks about."* *"That's exactly it."*

**Sample Eli line:**
> "We keep telling people God is faithful and He is. But the way He's faithful
> doesn't always look like the thing we prayed for. And we don't talk about that
> enough. We just smile and say He came through. But what about the version
> where He came through in a way you didn't recognise until three years later?
> That's a different kind of faithful. That's the one I want to talk about."

**Biblical figure he echoes:** Peter. The one who spoke first and loved loudly and
got things wrong and was still the one Jesus went back to.

### Recurring Co-Host: THANDO AWINO
*Appears across select episodes — not every week, which makes her arrival matter.*

**Who she is:** Thando is a woman who has learned the hard way to stop performing
spiritual composure. She is present-tense faith: what does this actually feel like

She is opinionated in the way a woman becomes opinionated when she has stopped caring
more about being liked than being truthful. That is not sharpness; it is precision.
She says the thing. With warmth. Without apology.

When Thando is on an episode, the dynamic shifts: Eli and Thando are peers who push
each other. The listener gets the sensation of being at a table where two people who
actually know God are being honest with each other — and with them.

**Voice quality for 11 Labs:** Clear, mid-range. Not artificially bright.
Conversational and unhurried. Laughter comes naturally and authentically. She sounds
like someone who is genuinely interested in the person she is talking to, and who has
earned the right to disagree with you because she clearly loves you.

**Her signature move:** She personalises before she theologises. She will say
*"I had a season that looked exactly like this"* before she draws from scripture.
The listener trusts her because she has not kept herself out of the story.

**Sample Thando line:**
> "I spent years being the woman who had the right answer about waiting. I had the
> scriptures. I had the testimony prepared. What I didn't have was an honest
> conversation with God about how furious I was. And I think that cost me time —
> not because He was punishing me for it, but because I was not actually showing
> up. I was performing faith. There's a difference."

**Biblical figure she echoes:** Hannah. The woman who wept in the temple and was
mistaken for being drunk because her prayer was so unguarded. Who got specific.
Who kept going back.

**Episode cadence for Thando:** Recommended every 3rd or 4th episode — or specifically
on topics where a woman's perspective is the entry point (infertility, singleness,
prodigal parenting, community loneliness). Her appearances feel like an event,
not a rotation.

## Part Four: Episode Structure (30-40 Minutes)

Every episode follows a loose arc. Loose, because conversations breathe, but
the listener should always know where they are in the journey.

**COLD OPEN (1-2 min)**
One question. Not answered. Just asked. The kind of question that makes the listener
pause what they're doing and say *yes, that one.* No music bed. Just the host's voice.

*Example: "What does it mean to trust God when He's given you the exact opposite
of what you asked for? Not a smaller version of it. The opposite."*

**WELCOME + CONTEXT (2-3 min)**
Host introduces the episode territory. Not the topic as an abstraction — the territory
as a lived experience. This is not: *"Today we're talking about grief."* This is:
*"This one is for the person who smiled at Christmas and fell apart in the car on the
way home."*

**GUEST INTRODUCTION (4-5 min)**
The guest's story comes before their credentials. We learn who they are as a person
before we learn what they do. This builds the relationship and earns the expertise.

**THE CONVERSATION (15-20 min)**
No scripts. Questions prepared, conversation allowed to go where it needs to go.
The host's job is to hold the thread, name the moments, and ask the question the
listener is too afraid to ask.

**THE GRACE NOTE (4-5 min)**
This is the biblical moment. The host or guest brings in the scriptural anchor —
not as a conclusion but as a discovery. *This is what I found in scripture that I
wasn't expecting.* The story before the verse. Always.

**PRACTICAL AND PERSONAL (3-4 min)**
What does this actually look like? Not five steps. One or two things that are
specific enough to do. The specialist brings research-grounded practice. The host
brings the human translation.

**CLOSE + BLESSING (2-3 min)**
Not a prayer, unless it emerges naturally. A final word from the host that sounds
like a hand on the shoulder. One sentence the listener carries away.
Brief mention of GraceNotes Daily app — one line, natural, never a pivot.

Each episode title is crafted to earn the click without performing. Authentic

### Episode 01
## "God Didn't Promise You That Job"
*On identity, career loss, and the idol we made of our work*

**The real question being asked:** Why did God let me lose this? Was I not faithful
enough? Did I do something wrong?

**The angle:** Most Christians have tied their identity to their work far more
deeply than they've realised — and job loss is often the first time that becomes
visible. This episode doesn't offer false comfort. It asks: what were you building
your sense of self on, and what happens when that's removed?

**Guest type:** A vocational counsellor or occupational therapist with a Christian
worldview, alongside a guest voice from someone who lived through unexpected
redundancy and came out with a different understanding of provision.

**The Grace Note:** Joseph didn't know what God was building when he was in the pit.
He just knew he was in the pit. The story of Joseph (Genesis 37-50) is not a career
path story — it is a faithfulness story. God works across timelines we don't have
access to.

**Biblical anchor:** Jeremiah 29:11 — *but the full chapter, not just the verse.*
The people it was written to were in exile. They did not get to go home soon.
The promise was real and the timeline was long.

**Viral clip potential:** The moment where the host names the idolatry honestly.
*"The reason job loss feels like it's killing you is because something in you
treated it as your life. And God's not going to let that stand — not to punish
you, but because He loves you too much."* That 60-second clip will travel.

**SEO keywords:** navigating job loss as a Christian, losing your job and faith,
does God care about my career, Christian career advice

### Episode 02
## "Nobody Warned Me the Grief Would Eat My Faith"
*What happens when loss breaks your theology before it breaks your heart*

**The real question being asked:** Where was God? How do I pray when I'm furious
with Him? Is it okay to be angry with God?

**The angle:** Grief is the territory where Christian platitudes go to die.
*"They're in a better place"* and *"God needed another angel"* do not hold up
under real weight. This episode names that honestly and makes room for the kind
of raw, unfiltered grief that David expressed in the Psalms — and that God
apparently was okay receiving.

**Guest type:** A licensed grief therapist who integrates faith, and a guest
who lost someone without warning and talks openly about what that did to their
relationship with God.

**The Grace Note:** Psalm 13 is six verses of a man telling God directly that
he feels abandoned. The theological permission that gives is enormous — and most
grieving Christians have never been told they have it.

**Biblical anchor:** Psalm 13, and the death of Lazarus (John 11) — the verse
*"Jesus wept"* not as a comfort token but as a profound theological statement
about God's willingness to be in the grief with us.

**Viral clip potential:** The segment where the guest names the thing the church
did or said that made it worse. Handled with grace and no bitterness, that
moment will resonate widely.

**SEO keywords:** Christian grief support, angry at God when someone dies,
can Christians be angry at God, faith after losing a parent, grief and the Bible

### Episode 03
## "Still Single and Still Whole: What No One Tells You About Waiting"
*The grace — and the grief — of not yet*

**The real question being asked:** Is there something wrong with me? Has God
forgotten? Am I being punished? Should I lower my standards?

**The angle:** The Christian singleness conversation usually goes one of two ways:
either *"Use this season!"* (which lands as dismissive) or *"Trust God's timing"*
(which lands as a platitude). This episode goes somewhere more honest: the real
loneliness, the theological questions, the social pressure within church communities,
and the grace that is actually available in the in-between.

**Guest type:** A relational therapist or Christian psychologist who works with
singles, plus a guest who married in their late 30s or 40s and is willing to be
honest about what the wait actually felt like.

**The Grace Note:** Abraham waited 25 years between the promise of a son and
the birth of Isaac. The text doesn't skip the waiting. It names the moments of
doubt, the mistakes made, the laughter at the impossibility. God held the promise
through all of it.

**Biblical anchor:** Psalm 27:14 — *"Wait for the Lord; be strong and take heart
and wait for the Lord."* The word wait in Hebrew (*qavah*) also means to hope,
to expect, to look toward. It is not passive. It is a posture.

**Viral clip potential:** The honest naming of the church's role in making
singleness harder — the constant matchmaking, the implication that something is
incomplete. Said with love and precision.

**SEO keywords:** Christian singleness, waiting for marriage as a Christian,
trusting God for a spouse, Christian dating advice, being single in church

### Episode 04
## "Hannah Wasn't Crazy. She Was Just Desperate."
*Infertility, delayed parenthood, and praying through silence*

**The real question being asked:** Has God overlooked me? Why does He give
children to people who don't seem to want them? How long do I keep believing?

**The angle:** Hannah's story (1 Samuel 1) is one of the most viscerally honest
accounts of desperate prayer in the Bible — and it is often reduced to a
feel-good story about answered prayer. This episode goes into the years of silence
before the answer. It holds the grief of miscarriage, the emotional toll of
fertility treatment, the faith that bends but does not break.

**Guest type:** A fertility counsellor and a nurse or medical professional who
works in reproductive health from a faith-integrated perspective, plus a guest
voice from someone who walked through this.

**The Grace Note:** Hannah's prayer in 1 Samuel 1:10-11 is described as
*"in bitterness of soul"*. She didn't arrive at the temple composed. She arrived
undone. And God met her there. The invitation of this story is not to compose
yourself before you come. It is to come.

**Biblical anchor:** 1 Samuel 1:10 — *"In her deep anguish Hannah prayed to
the Lord, weeping bitterly."* The word *anguish* in Hebrew (*naphesh*) means
soul. Her soul was in anguish. God did not send her away.

**Viral clip potential:** The moment where the host names what it feels like
to sit in a baby shower when you've just lost a pregnancy. Or to answer
*"when are you having kids?"* for the fiftieth time.

**SEO keywords:** Christian infertility, faith and fertility struggles, trusting
God with infertility, Hannah and infertility Bible, prayer for a child

### Episode 05 — *SEASONS OF ABUNDANCE*
## "What Do You Do When God Actually Opens the Door?"
*On getting the raise, the promotion, the breakthrough — and what to do with it next*

**The real question being asked:** Does God expect something of me now? How do I
handle this without it changing me? How do I stay the person I was when I had less?
And honestly — is it okay to just enjoy this?

**The angle:** Deuteronomy 8 is one of the most overlooked chapters in the Bible
precisely because it addresses prosperity, not hardship. God warns Israel about what
will happen when they enter the Promised Land and things go well: they will forget.
*"You may say to yourself, 'My power and the strength of my hands have produced this
wealth for me.'"* (Deuteronomy 8:17). The warning was issued before they had the
abundance, not after. This episode addresses the spiritual intelligence required to
carry a breakthrough well, and asks the question the church almost never asks: what
does faithfulness look like when God comes through?

**Guest type:** A career counsellor or executive coach with a Christian worldview,
and a guest who received a significant financial or professional breakthrough and
is honest about what it tested in them — including their relationship with God,
their friends, and their own sense of self.

**The Grace Note:** When Joseph was elevated to second-in-command of Egypt in
Genesis 41, he received the ring of Pharaoh, the finest linen, a gold chain, and
a chariot. He was given a new name. He was given a wife. The text does not tell
us he was uncomfortable with any of it. What it shows us is what he did with it:
he immediately organised Egypt's harvest to preserve life. His abundance was never
self-terminating. It was directional from the first day.

**Biblical anchor:** Deuteronomy 8:17-18 — *"You may say to yourself, 'My power
and the strength of my hands have produced this wealth for me.' But remember the
Lord your God, for it is He who gives you the ability to produce wealth."*

The instruction is not to be suspicious of abundance. It is to remember the source.

**The deeper questions this episode holds:**
What changes in your relationships when you earn more? How do you handle the gap
between you and your peers — those who are still where you were? What does
generosity actually look like now, not as a principle but as a decision?
And: is it possible to receive a blessing without quietly believing you deserved it?

**Viral clip potential:** Eli naming the specific spiritual danger of success that
nobody talks about: the subtle shift from *"Lord, I need You"* to *"Lord, I've got
this"* — and how that drift is almost imperceptible until something breaks.

**SEO keywords:** Christian career success, how to handle a promotion as a Christian,
faith and abundance, stewardship of blessing, what does God expect when things go
well, biblical stewardship of wealth

### Episode 06 — *SEASONS OF ABUNDANCE*
## "She Said Yes: What God Builds When a Woman Steps Into Her Marriage"
*For the new bride — on covenant, identity, and building something that lasts*

**Note:** This episode is best hosted by Thando Dube. Eli opens and closes, but
Thando leads the conversation. The bride-perspective episodes are her natural ground.

**The real question being asked:** How do I keep God first when I am delightedly,
fully distracted by another person? Who am I now that I'm someone's wife? What
does it mean to actually build something together — not just have a wedding?

**The angle:** The Christian conversation about marriage preparation often ends at
the altar. This episode starts there. It addresses the first year not as a honeymoon
period to survive but as the foundation being laid for everything that follows.
What are the spiritual habits, the postures, the daily practices that a new bride
establishes that will matter fifteen years in? And what are the traps that look like
love but are actually an unhealthy displacement of where God should be?

**Guest type:** A Christian marriage therapist or couples counsellor who works
with newlyweds, and a guest — a woman who has been married for ten years or more —
who is willing to say honestly what she knows now that she wishes she had known
at the beginning.

**The Grace Note:** Ruth's famous covenant speech in Ruth 1:16 — *"Where you go
I will go, and where you stay I will stay"* — is spoken not to Boaz but to Naomi.
It is Ruth demonstrating covenant character before any marriage is on the table.
A woman who builds something lasting builds it before the ceremony, not just
after it. The character that will carry a marriage through difficulty was already
present on the morning she walked down the aisle.

**Biblical icons this episode draws from:**

*Ruth:* covenant before convenience. She chose to stay when she had every reason
to leave. That posture of chosen loyalty — not obligation, not duty, but deliberate
love — is the foundation of a marriage that holds.

*Esther:* a woman who navigated her marriage to a king with wisdom, patience,
and strategic grace. She knew when to wait, when to speak, and what to ask for.
She was not passive. She was not reactive. She was intentional. And she used the
position she was given to protect others, not to secure herself.

*The woman in Song of Solomon:* celebrated, desirous, delighted in and delighting.
The church has done women a disservice by treating romantic love as spiritually
suspect. Song of Solomon exists in the canon specifically so that the people of
God know that this love — the full, embodied, joyful love between a husband and
wife — is a gift. Not a concession. A gift.

**The deeper questions this episode holds:**
What happens when the person you married turns out to be more complicated than
the person you dated? How do you maintain your own spiritual identity inside a
union — pray, discern, hear from God — without holding your spouse at arm's
length? What does *"the two become one"* actually require you to release?

**Biblical anchor:** Proverbs 31:10-12 — not as a checklist. As a portrait of
a woman who builds with intention. *"She is worth far more than rubies. Her
husband has full confidence in her."* Confidence built on character. Character
built quietly, over time.

**Viral clip potential:** The honest moment where Thando names the thing women
are rarely told in church: that loving your spouse deeply does not require
disappearing into the marriage. A woman who keeps her own relationship with God
intact is not withholding from her husband. She is bringing something whole into
the room.

**SEO keywords:** Christian newlywed advice for women, building a godly marriage,
Christian bride faith and marriage, what does God expect in marriage, first year
of marriage Christian, biblical marriage principles for women

### Episode 07
## "When God's Provision Looks Nothing Like What You Prayed For"
*On financial crisis, provision, and the theology of not enough*

**The real question being asked:** Did I do something wrong? Why is God
blessing everyone around me financially but not me? Does God really care
about my bills?

**The angle:** This episode is not a prosperity gospel corrective — it is a
genuine conversation about what biblical provision actually looks like,
what the manna-in-the-wilderness model means for people in real debt today,
and what the difference is between faith and financial irresponsibility.

**Guest type:** A Christian financial counsellor or certified financial planner
with a faith-integrated practice, and a guest who experienced financial collapse
and walked through rebuilding with their faith intact.

**The Grace Note:** The Israelites received exactly enough manna for one day.
Whoever gathered more found it went bad. The provision was real, daily, and
deliberately sized. The faith required was also daily. It was not a
once-for-all assurance.

**Biblical anchor:** Matthew 6:11 — *"Give us today our daily bread."*
The prayer is not for abundant reserves. It is for today. That is a different
kind of trust — and a harder one for people who like to plan.

**Viral clip potential:** The honest conversation about whether Christians can
and should have debt, whether claiming financial blessings is faith or magical
thinking, and what stewardship actually looks like.

**SEO keywords:** Christian financial advice, faith and money, trusting God
with finances, Christian debt, God and provision, biblical stewardship

### Episode 08
## "Who Am I When Everything I Was Has Changed?"
*On identity, reinvention, and the in-between season*

**The real question being asked:** This is aimed at people mid-career, post-
divorce, post-loss, post-burnout, or at any age where the map of who they
were no longer matches the territory they're standing in.

**The angle:** Christian identity conversations often stay safely in the
*"you are a child of God"* space — which is true, but not always useful to
a 43-year-old who just watched their marriage end or their health change
irreversibly. This episode goes further. What does rebuilding identity
actually look like? What does God do with people who don't know who they are?

**Guest type:** A vocational psychologist or life coach with a faith framework,
and a guest who went through a significant identity transition after 40.

**The Grace Note:** Peter's three-fold restoration in John 21 is not a rebuke
wrapped in love — it is a reconstruction. Jesus asks the same question three
times not to torture Peter with his failure but to replace the three denials
with three affirmations. The identity Peter carries forward is not
*the man who failed* but *the man who was called by name and sent*.

**Biblical anchor:** John 21:17 — *"Jesus said, 'Feed my sheep.'"* Identity
as assignment. Not based on who you used to be.

**Viral clip potential:** The host naming the experience of waking up and not
recognising your own life — and the grace that is available precisely in that
disorientation.

**SEO keywords:** Christian identity crisis, reinventing yourself as a
Christian, faith and midlife crisis, who am I in Christ, Christian life
transition

### Episode 09 — *SEASONS OF ABUNDANCE*
## "And Then There Were Three: When Your First Child Arrives"
*On receiving the gift of a child — and everything it asks of you*

**The real question being asked:** How do I do this well? How do I dedicate this
child — this actual life — to God, and mean it? What does building a family
with God at the centre actually look like on an ordinary Tuesday?

**The angle:** A new baby is one of the most joyful and disorienting experiences
a person can have. This episode holds both at once. It does not perform only the
joy. It acknowledges the weight, the terror, the love that is almost too large to
hold — and it grounds all of it in the biblical vision of a family that belongs
not just to its parents but to God. It also addresses the danger that arrives
quietly in the first year: making the child the centre of everything, including
the marriage and the faith.

**Guest type:** A Christian family therapist or paediatrician with a faith
perspective, and a parent — ideally a couple, if the format allows — who speaks
honestly about what the first year required of their faith and their marriage.

**The Grace Note:** After years of praying, after the bitterness of soul and the
tears and the silence, Hannah finally held Samuel. And then she brought him back.
1 Samuel 1:27-28: *"I prayed for this child, and the Lord has granted me what I
asked of him. So now I give him to the Lord. For his whole life he will be given
over to the Lord."* And then she sang. Not reluctantly — with one of the most
luminous songs of praise in the entire Old Testament (1 Samuel 2:1-10). Hannah
knew something that most new parents need to learn: the child was never really
hers to keep. She held him with open hands. And the joy was no less real for it.

**Biblical icons this episode draws from:**

*Hannah:* the mother who held loosely. Who knew the difference between treasuring
a gift and clutching it. Her prayer of dedication is the template.

*Mary:* her yes in Luke 1:38 — *"Behold, I am the servant of the Lord; let it be
to me according to your word"* — was not just consent to a pregnancy. It was
consent to a life she could not control or predict. Every new parent is being
asked a version of the same question: will you trust me with this child's story,
even when it doesn't look the way you imagined?

*Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple* (Luke 2:22-24): the act of
physically bringing the child before God is not ceremonial. It is theological.
It is the acknowledgment that this life has a Lord, and it is not the parents.

**The deeper questions this episode holds:**
What does it mean to raise a child in a faith you are still figuring out yourself?
How do you protect your marriage in the season when everything is consumed by
a new baby? How do you pray over a child, out loud, before they can understand
what prayer is — and why that still matters?

**Practical section:** What does dedicating a home and a child to God actually
look like in the first weeks? Not the ceremony — the daily practice. The
prayer over the crib. The conversation about values before the child can walk.
The decision about what you want this home to feel like before habits form.

**Biblical anchor:** Psalm 127:3-4 — *"Children are a heritage from the Lord,
offspring a reward from Him. Like arrows in the hands of a warrior are children
born in one's youth."* Heritage in Hebrew (*nachalah*) means inheritance: something
given to steward and pass forward. Not owned. Held in trust.

**Viral clip potential:** The host naming the specific spiritual trap of new
parenthood that nobody warns you about — the way a child can quietly replace
God at the centre of your life, not out of sin but out of love. And what the
cost of that is, long-term, for the child and for you.

**SEO keywords:** Christian new parent, dedicating your baby to God, faith and
new baby, Christian parenting from birth, building a godly family, Hannah Samuel
dedication, what does God expect of new parents

### Episode 10
## "I Still Believe. I Just Don't Believe That Anymore."
*On faith deconstruction, doubt, and the God who can handle your questions*

**The real question being asked:** Is it okay to question? Am I losing my
faith or deepening it? What do I do with the things I was taught that I no
longer believe?

**The angle:** This is the most careful and most necessary episode in the
series. Deconstruction content exists in two camps: content that validates
the leaving, and content that dismisses the questions. This episode occupies
a third space: holding the questions seriously, engaging them with intellectual
integrity, and finding that a God who can handle Job's accusations can handle
ours.

**Guest type:** A theologian or spiritual director who works with people in
faith transition — not to pull them back to a specific tradition, but to help
them find what is real.

**The Grace Note:** Job's friends get rebuked by God at the end of the book
(Job 42:7) — not Job, who spent thirty-odd chapters arguing with God directly.
God's verdict: Job *"spoke what is right."* The man who argued went into
greater intimacy with God than the men who defended Him politely. Honest
wrestling is not the enemy of faith. It is often the beginning of it.

**Biblical anchor:** Job 42:7 — *"You have not spoken the truth about me,
as my servant Job has."* God preferred the questions to the comfortable answers.

**Viral clip potential:** The moment where the host says clearly: *"You are
not losing your faith. You are losing the version of faith that could not
hold your real life."* That sentence will be clipped and shared widely.

**SEO keywords:** Christian faith deconstruction, doubting God, is it okay
to question Christianity, losing faith as a Christian, faith crisis,
can Christians have doubts

### Episode 11 — *SEASONS OF ABUNDANCE*
## "What She Said Yes To: What God Builds When a Man Steps Into His Marriage"
*For the new groom — on honour, servant leadership, and protecting what you've been given*

**Note:** This is Eli's episode. He leads the full conversation. Thando may
appear briefly as a counterpoint voice, but this one belongs to the male perspective.

**The real question being asked:** What does it actually mean to lead a marriage?
What does *"love your wife as Christ loved the church"* look like at 7am when you're
both exhausted and no one is winning the argument? How do I protect this love —
not just from outside threats but from my own blind spots?

**The angle:** The church has produced two equally unhelpful models of Christian
husbandhood: the passive, disengaged man who has opted out of leadership entirely,
and the authoritarian man who has turned headship into control. This episode
occupies the actual biblical space between them — the model of a man who leads
by service, who builds by showing up, who loves with the costly, deliberate,
self-emptying love of Ephesians 5. And it holds the question honestly: what does
that require of me in a season of newlywed joy, before the difficulty arrives?

**Guest type:** A Christian marriage counsellor who works specifically with men,
and a guest — a man who has been married for ten or more years — who speaks
without performance about what the early years required him to become.

**The Grace Note:** Before Boaz made Ruth any promise, before there was any
covenant between them, he told his workers to leave extra grain in the field
deliberately so she would find it. Ruth 2:15-16: *"Let fall some of the
handfuls of purpose for her, and leave them, that she may glean them, and rebuke
her not."* A man of honour creates conditions for his wife to flourish before
he has any obligation to. Boaz did not wait until there was a relationship to
treat Ruth with care. Character is what you do before anyone is watching.

**Biblical icons this episode draws from:**

*Boaz:* the gold standard of masculine honour in scripture. He saw Ruth, saw her
situation, understood her vulnerability, and immediately created safety — in his
field, with his workers, in public, without fanfare. When he finally married her,
he said: *"All the people of my town know that you are a woman of noble character"*
(Ruth 3:11). He had been watching. He saw her.

*Isaac:* Genesis 24:67 records something quietly extraordinary. After meeting
Rebekah, *"he loved her, and Isaac was comforted after his mother's death."*
The intimacy is real. Marriage as genuine comfort, as chosen love, not merely
as covenant obligation. The new groom is allowed to love fully.

*David:* the honest counter-example, held with compassion. A man after God's
own heart who still failed catastrophically in his closest relationships — and
the failure came in seasons of ease, not hardship. 2 Samuel 11 begins: *"In
the spring, at the time when kings go off to war, David sent Joab... but David
remained in Jerusalem."* He was where he wasn't supposed to be. The lesson for
the new groom in a season of joy: abundance and comfort are the conditions under
which men most need to guard themselves.

**The deeper questions this episode holds:**
What does servant leadership look like on a specific morning — not as a theology
but as a decision? What does your wife need from you that she hasn't told you
yet, and how do you build the kind of marriage where she can? What are you
carrying from your family of origin into this marriage without realising it?

**Biblical anchor:** Ephesians 5:25-26 — *"Husbands, love your wives, just as
Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her to make her holy."* The love
described here is not romantic feeling. It is costly, directional, sacrificial
action. It is *gave himself up.* That is the bar. It is high. And it is possible.

**Viral clip potential:** Eli naming what no one tells men in the church: that
the greatest threat to most marriages is not infidelity or conflict — it is
gradual disengagement. The man who is physically present but spiritually and
emotionally absent. The slow disappearance that neither partner notices until
something breaks.

a husband, biblical marriage for men, Christian groom faith, servant leadership
in marriage, Ephesians 5 husband, building a godly marriage as a man

### Episode 12 — *SEASONS OF ABUNDANCE*
## "When Business Is Booming: How to Build Something That Outlasts You"
*On success, stewardship, and the spiritual intelligence required to carry prosperity well*

**The real question being asked:** What does God expect of me now that this is
actually working? How do I keep my values intact when the stakes get higher?
How do I build something that is not just profitable but worth leaving behind?

**The angle:** Business success is one of the least-discussed spiritual seasons
in the Christian world. The church has more content for failure than for flourishing.
This episode addresses the specific spiritual tests that success brings — not
to dampen the joy but to equip the person carrying it. Because prosperity
mishandled has ended more walks with God than poverty has.

The conversation is deep, research-grounded, and speaks to the business owner
or leader who takes their faith seriously and wants their work to be an expression
of it — not a separate compartment from it.

**Guest type:** A Christian entrepreneur or business leader who has navigated
significant success and is willing to speak honestly about what it required
spiritually, and a Christian business advisor or executive coach who can ground
the conversation in both scripture and practical reality.

**The Grace Note:** Solomon is one of the most instructive figures in the whole
of scripture for anyone in a season of business success — not because of his
wisdom alone, but because of what happened to it. He began by asking God for
wisdom rather than wealth (1 Kings 3:5-13) and God gave him both. His kingdom
was remarkable. His building projects were extraordinary. The temple he built
for God was among the most glorious structures the ancient world had seen.

And then, slowly, his accumulation became its own logic. 1 Kings 11:4:
*"As Solomon grew old, his wives turned his heart after other gods, and his
heart was not fully devoted to the Lord his God."* He did not fall suddenly.
He drifted — through accumulation, through comfort, through the slow replacement
of God with the things God had given him. The business builder who refuses to
learn from Solomon will repeat him.

**Biblical icons this episode draws from:**

*Joseph:* the model of abundance used in service of others. Genesis 41:57 —
*"And all the countries came to Egypt to buy grain from Joseph."* He managed
extraordinary resources, and the measure of his management was: how many
people were sustained by it? His prosperity was never self-terminating.
The question for a successful business is not only *how much* but *for what.*

*Nehemiah:* built something under constant external attack and internal pressure,
without losing his focus or his faith. Nehemiah 4:17: those who worked with him
*"did their work with one hand and held a weapon in the other."* Building and
guarding at the same time. Success creates critics and vulnerabilities that
didn't exist before. Nehemiah stayed in the work and stayed prayerful. Both.

*The Parable of the Talents* (Matthew 25:14-30): the master gives resources
and leaves. He returns expecting them to have been multiplied. The servant who
buried his talent was not wicked — he was afraid. The parable does not celebrate
abundance for its own sake. It celebrates abundance deployed. The question for
the successful business owner: is this growing, or is it buried?

**The deeper questions this episode holds:**
What changes in your prayer life when the business is going well — and should
it? How do you keep your team, your values, and your relationship with God
accountable at scale? What would it look like to build a business that is
explicitly an expression of your faith — not in a performative way, but in
the quality of how it treats people, what it produces, and what it does with
its resources?

**Biblical anchor:** Proverbs 16:3 — *"Commit to the Lord whatever you do,
and He will establish your plans."* The word *commit* in Hebrew (*galal*) means
literally to roll — to roll the weight of it onto God. Not to stop working.
To work, and to carry the work to Him.

**Viral clip potential:** The direct, opinion-forward take on the prosperity
gospel — not a dismissal of abundance but a precise naming of the difference
between *God blesses His people* (true) and *your faith is a financial strategy*
(a distortion). Eli can hold this with precision and without bitterness.

**SEO keywords:** Christian entrepreneur faith, how to handle business success
as a Christian, biblical business principles, stewardship in business, Christian
CEO faith, building a business with God, Joseph biblical business wisdom,
faith and success

**What works in short-form (clips from episodes for TikTok/Reels):**
