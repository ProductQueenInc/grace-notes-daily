# The Job Is Gone. Your Faith Doesn't Have to Go With It.

*By GraceNotes Daily | Between Sundays, Season 1 Episode 1*

---

She lost her first job at 27. Restructure. Nothing personal, they said, and she believed them because what else do you do with something like that. She had her CV updated within a week. She was back to work in a few months. She was fine.

Then she lost the next one at 28.

This one was harder to explain, harder to hold, harder to describe to her mother over the phone. She picked her words carefully. Made it sound smaller than it was. Told herself it was just bad luck, that these things happen, that God had something better coming.

At 29 she lost a third job.

By the time she sat in a car park after being let go for the fourth time in four years, at 30, she wasn't reaching for explanations anymore. She was just very, very tired. And for the first time, she didn't open her phone to call someone. She didn't pray. She sat in the silence and realised, with a kind of sad clarity, that she wasn't sure she knew how to talk to God about this anymore.

---

## It's Not Just About the Job

If you have ever been in a season like that one, you know it isn't only about the job.

The job is the thing that happened. What happens inside you is something else entirely. It's the specific embarrassment of updating your LinkedIn again and hoping nobody notices. It's the way you start editing what you say at dinner, describing your situation in the vaguest possible terms so you don't have to watch someone's face try to arrange itself into the right expression. It's the internal monologue that starts harmlessly enough, *this is temporary, God has a plan*, and then somewhere around month three or four starts asking harder questions.

Did I do something wrong? Is this a punishment? I prayed about this, I thought God said yes, so what happened?

These are real questions. They deserve to be named, not managed.

Here is what doesn't always get said honestly about job loss: it does something specific to your sense of who you are. Not just what you do, but who you are. Because somewhere along the way, most of us let our work carry things it was never supposed to carry. Our sense of value. Our sense of direction. Our quiet conviction that God was pleased with us. And when the job goes, so does the scaffolding. What's left standing underneath is a question you may not have known you were avoiding: who am I when I'm not this?

---

## What the Bible Says About Seasons Like This

There's a man in the Bible named Elijah who, by any measure, was one of the most effective people of his generation. Prophet. The man who called down fire from heaven in front of an entire nation and watched as God answered. And then, almost immediately after that peak moment, he found himself sitting under a tree in the desert asking God to let him die.

He was done. Burned out, afraid, convinced he was the only one left who believed in anything, and that it hadn't been worth it.

God's response was not what most people would have written for Him.

He didn't send a vision. He didn't replay the highlights. An angel appeared, touched Elijah on the shoulder, and said simply: get up and eat. There was bread. There was water. And then the angel came back a second time and said something that stays with you once you've read it: the journey is too much for you. He didn't question Elijah's faith or tell him to pull himself together. He just said: this is too much. Here is food. Rest.

That phrase, the journey is too much for you, is one of the most tender things God says anywhere in scripture. And it was spoken to a man in complete spiritual and emotional collapse, not to someone performing faithfulness correctly.

If that is where you are right now, that word is for you too.

---

## The Provision That Doesn't Look Like Provision

The woman from the car park eventually came back to faith. Not dramatically. Not at an altar call or after a thunderclap of revelation. One ordinary afternoon, quietly, without announcement, something settled.

She started a business. Then another. One day she looked around and realised she was building something she actually believed in, and that the four years of loss had given her something the good years couldn't have: a faith that had been tested and chosen, not just inherited and assumed.

She wouldn't choose those four years again. But she wouldn't undo them either.

One of the stories that kept her company during that season was from 1 Kings 17. Elijah, still in hiding, is fed by ravens, birds that Jewish law classified as unclean, about as far from a conventional symbol of divine provision as you could get. They came twice a day, every day, with bread and meat. God used the last source anyone would have expected. The provision was real, it was consistent, and it came through a channel nobody would have prayed for specifically.

If you are waiting for provision to arrive through the door you have been watching, it is worth knowing that God has a long history of coming through a different one entirely.

*"Consider the ravens,"* Jesus said to his disciples, who were anxious about the same things people are always anxious about. *"They do not sow or reap, they have no storeroom or barn; yet God feeds them."* He was making the same point centuries later that He made for Elijah: the provision is real, and the source may surprise you.

---

## What You Can Actually Do Today

Your anger is allowed. Your questions are allowed. God is not fragile and He is not surprised by what you're carrying. David wrote in Psalm 13: *how long, Lord? Will you forget me forever?* That line is in the Bible. God left it there. He is fine with the question. Be honest with Him, even if honest sounds like: I don't know how to talk to you right now.

Tell someone. Not for advice, not for a solution, just so you are not carrying it alone in the dark. The hiding makes it heavier. It always does.

And watch for provision that comes sideways. The phone call from someone you haven't spoken to in two years. The unexpected connection. The opportunity that arrives through a door you didn't know to knock on. It may not look like what you prayed for. It may be exactly what you needed.

The job is gone. But you are not.

Psalm 34:18 says the Lord is close to the brokenhearted. That word, close, means present in the room with you, not just willing to come if called. It means now, today, in the car park, in the silence you haven't found a way to break yet.

He is already there.
