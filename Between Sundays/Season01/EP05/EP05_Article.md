# When the Answer Finally Comes: What God Expects of You in a Season of Abundance

*By GraceNotes Daily | Between Sundays, Season 1 Episode 5*

---

Beni ran a logistics consultancy for years before it actually worked. That's the honest version. The longer version is that he built it from Nairobi, worked it through seasons when the money wasn't quite there and the momentum wasn't quite there either, prayed about it more than he talked about it, and somewhere in his late thirties started wondering whether he had read things wrong. Whether the thing he was building was actually what he was supposed to be building.

He was forty-one when a contract came in that changed the scale of things permanently. The same year, he got married.

He has thought carefully about that timing. His take is not what you might expect from someone telling a breakthrough story. He doesn't think God was withholding the success to teach him a lesson. He thinks he simply wasn't ready to carry it well before then. That the man who received the contract at forty-one was different from the man who would have received it at thirty-one, and that the difference mattered more than the contract did.

That question is what this piece is actually about.

---

## What Nobody Warns You About When Things Go Right

There is a great deal of Christian content about how to trust God when things are hard. There is almost none about what to do when He answers.

This is worth sitting with, because abundance has its own specific tests and they are different from the tests that come with hardship. When things are hard, you know you need God. You feel the need clearly. You pray with urgency. There is no pretending you are fine when you are not.

Abundance does something quieter, and in some ways more dangerous. It fills the space where need used to live. Slowly, without announcement, the urgency leaves your prayers. The tone shifts. You still talk to God, but the conversations start to sound a little more like updates and a little less like dependence. You are not turning away from Him. You are just, gradually, turning slightly.

That drift, that almost imperceptible turn, is exactly what the God of Deuteronomy 8 was trying to prevent.

---

## The Warning That Came Before the Blessing

One of the most striking things about Deuteronomy 8 is that it was written as a warning to people who did not yet have the thing they were being warned about.

The Israelites were on the edge of the Promised Land. They had not entered it yet. They had not yet built the houses or planted the vineyards or accumulated the silver and gold that God was about to give them. And God, before any of it arrived, said: when you have eaten and are satisfied, when you have built fine houses and settled down, when your herds grow large and your silver and gold increase and your heart becomes proud, do not forget the Lord your God.

And then, a few verses later: you may say to yourself, my power and the strength of my hands have produced this wealth for me. But remember the Lord your God, for it is He who gives you the ability to produce wealth.

God knew what prosperity would do. He had watched it happen before. He issued the warning before the abundance arrived because after it arrives, warnings feel less urgent.

The question the passage keeps asking is a simple one: when things go well, who do you think made that happen?

---

## What Joseph Did With the Ring

In Genesis 41, Pharaoh places his own signet ring on Joseph's finger. Fine linen, a gold chain, the second royal chariot. Joseph goes from prison to palace in a single morning.

What Joseph does with the power is what makes the story worth telling. He immediately organises seven years of abundant harvest so that the famine already coming will not destroy the nation. He builds systems designed to feed other people, not to accumulate for himself. The abundance given to him became, in almost the same breath, something he used in service of others.

The story makes that seem natural. It was not. It was a character Joseph had built over years in the pit and in the prison, long before the ring arrived. The ring was given to the man he had already become.

---

## The Caution in Solomon's Story

Solomon's story is harder to sit with, because most of us are more like Solomon than we are like Joseph.

Solomon started by asking God for wisdom rather than wealth. It is one of the more genuinely moving moments in the Old Testament, a young king with everything available to him choosing the thing that would help him steward everything else well. God gave him wisdom and gave him wealth alongside it, and the early years of his reign were everything they were meant to be.

Then the accumulation took on its own logic. More horses, more political marriages, more alliances built on things other than faithfulness to God. By 1 Kings 11, the text says it plainly: Solomon's heart was not fully devoted to the Lord his God.

He did not decide one morning to walk away. The gifts accumulated until there was simply less room for the giver. That is how it usually goes.

---

## What Faithfulness in Abundance Actually Looks Like

Beni said something once that has stayed with me. He said: I am not a more faithful person because things went well. I think I was ready for things to go well because of what the long years built in me. And I know the difference. I have seen what happens to people who got everything they wanted before they were ready for it.

Faithfulness in abundance is not a feeling. It is a daily decision made in ordinary moments that feel too small to be spiritual.

It looks like keeping the prayer life you had when you needed it, even when the urgency has gone. The conversation with God was never supposed to be proportional to how much you currently need from Him.

It looks like staying accountable to someone who knew you before. Someone who can say, without much ceremony, that you seem different lately.

It looks like asking what this abundance is for beyond your own household. Joseph asked it. The answer shaped everything he built with the power he was given.

Proverbs 16:3 says: commit to the Lord whatever you do, and He will establish your plans. The Hebrew word for commit there means to roll, to physically roll the weight of something onto another person. Keep working. Keep planning. But carry the work back to God regularly and put it in His hands.

The breakthrough came. Sit in that gratitude fully, without guilt. God is not asking you to be suspicious of the blessing or to make yourself small in response to it.

He is asking you to remember where it came from. And to make sure that when the next season arrives, you are still the person who knows how to ask.
